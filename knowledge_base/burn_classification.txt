SOURCE: American Burn Association. Advanced Burn Life Support Course Provider Manual. 2018.
TOPIC: Classification of Burn Depth and Severity

BURN DEPTH CLASSIFICATION:

1. FIRST DEGREE (Superficial / Epidermal):
   - Involves epidermis only
   - Appearance: Red, dry, no blisters
   - Pain: Painful, sensitive to touch
   - Healing: 3-5 days, no scarring
   - Example: Sunburn
   - NOT counted in TBSA for fluid calculation

2. SECOND DEGREE - Superficial Partial Thickness:
   - Involves epidermis and superficial dermis (papillary dermis)
   - Appearance: Red, moist, blistered, weeping
   - Pain: Very painful, hyperesthetic
   - Healing: 10-14 days, minimal scarring
   - Counted in TBSA

3. SECOND DEGREE - Deep Partial Thickness:
   - Involves epidermis and deep dermis (reticular dermis)
   - Appearance: Red to pale pink, wet or dry, may or may not blister
   - Pain: Less painful (nerve damage)
   - Healing: 3-8 weeks, significant scarring, may need grafting
   - Counted in TBSA

4. THIRD DEGREE (Full Thickness):
   - Destroys entire dermis, may involve subcutaneous tissue
   - Appearance: White, brown, or black; leathery, dry, no blisters
   - Pain: Painless (nerve endings destroyed)
   - Healing: Does NOT heal without skin grafting
   - Counted in TBSA

5. FOURTH DEGREE:
   - Extends to bone, muscle, or tendon
   - Requires amputation or extensive debridement
   - Counted in TBSA

SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION (American Burn Association):

MINOR BURNS:
- Adults: < 10% TBSA partial thickness, < 2% full thickness
- Children/Elderly: < 5% TBSA partial thickness
- No involvement of face, hands, feet, genitalia, or major joints

MODERATE BURNS:
- Adults: 10-20% TBSA partial thickness, 2-5% full thickness
- Children: 5-10% TBSA

MAJOR BURNS (require burn center referral):
- > 20% TBSA in adults
- > 10% TBSA in children under 10 or adults over 50
- > 5% full thickness burns
- Burns involving face, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum, major joints
- Electrical burns
- Chemical burns
- Inhalation injury
- Circumferential burns of limbs or chest

CRITICAL/LIFE-THREATENING:
- TBSA > 40%
- Associated inhalation injury
- Significant comorbidities
