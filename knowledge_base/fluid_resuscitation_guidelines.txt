---
SOURCE: Romanowski KS, et al. American Burn Association Guidelines on the Management of Acute 
Burns. J Burn Care Res. 2023;44(3):481-500.
TOPIC: Current Guidelines for Burn Fluid Resuscitation

INITIAL ASSESSMENT (ABCDE):
A - Airway: Assess for inhalation injury, stridor, carbonaceous sputum
B - Breathing: Respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, chest burns (escharotomy if needed)
C - Circulation: IV access (2 large-bore IVs), fluid resuscitation
D - Disability: Neurological status, Glasgow Coma Scale
E - Exposure: Remove clothing, estimate burn size and depth

FLUID RESUSCITATION PRINCIPLES:
1. Start fluid resuscitation for burns > 15% TBSA in adults, > 10% in children
2. Use Lactated Ringer's as first-line crystalloid
3. Avoid colloids in first 8-12 hours (increased capillary permeability)
4. After 24 hours, may add albumin 5% to reduce total crystalloid volume
5. Avoid dextrose-containing solutions in adults (hyperglycemia risk)

ENDPOINTS OF RESUSCITATION:
Primary endpoint: Urine output
- Adults: 0.5 mL/kg/hour (30-50 mL/hour)
- Children < 30 kg: 1 mL/kg/hour

Secondary endpoints:
- Mean arterial pressure > 65 mmHg
- Lactate clearance
- Base excess > -6

OVER-RESUSCITATION (Fluid Creep):
Common complication when too much fluid given.
Complications: Pulmonary edema, abdominal compartment syndrome, extremity compartment syndrome.
Prevention: Titrate to urine output, avoid bolus fluids, consider vitamin C 66 mg/kg/hr 
in first 24 hours (reduces capillary leak).

SPECIAL SITUATIONS:
- Electrical burns: Increase fluid to maintain urine output 1 mL/kg/hr 
  (myoglobinuria risk -> alkalinize urine)
- Chemical burns: Copious irrigation first, then standard resuscitation
- Inhalation injury: Increase fluid estimate by 30-40%
- Circumferential burns: Monitor for compartment syndrome, escharotomy if needed

VASOPRESSORS:
- Not first-line in burn resuscitation
- Use only if adequate fluid given but hypotension persists
- Norepinephrine is agent of choice if needed
