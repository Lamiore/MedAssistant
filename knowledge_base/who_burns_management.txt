TOPIC: WHO Guidelines and Global Perspective on Burn Management

GLOBAL BURDEN:
- ~180,000 deaths per year from burns globally
- 95% of burn deaths occur in low and middle-income countries
- Burns are a leading cause of disability in Southeast Asia and Africa
- Indonesia: burns rank among top causes of trauma-related morbidity

WHO FIRST AID RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. STOP the burning: remove from source, stop/drop/roll
2. COOL: cool burn with cool running water for 20 minutes (NOT ice)
3. COVER: with clean non-fluffy material
4. CALM: reassure patient, keep warm to prevent hypothermia
5. REFER: to appropriate health facility

DO NOT:
- Apply ice (causes vasoconstriction and deeper injury)
- Apply toothpaste, butter, oils (infection risk)
- Break blisters (infection risk)
- Apply tight bandages

WHO TRIAGE FOR BURN PATIENTS (in resource-limited settings):
IMMEDIATE (Red):
- TBSA > 20% with hemodynamic instability
- Airway burns with respiratory distress
- Circumferential chest burns

URGENT (Yellow):
- TBSA 10-20%, stable
- Partial thickness burns to critical areas

DELAYED (Green):
- TBSA < 10%, no critical areas
- First degree burns

EXPECTANT (Black):
- TBSA > 60% in resource-limited settings
- Severe inhalation injury + TBSA > 40%

INFECTION PREVENTION:
- Clean burns with mild soap and water or chlorhexidine
- Silver sulfadiazine or petroleum gauze for wound coverage
- Tetanus prophylaxis for unimmunized patients
- Systemic antibiotics NOT recommended prophylactically (promotes resistance)

NUTRITIONAL SUPPORT:
- Burns are hypermetabolic injuries
- Early enteral feeding within 6 hours of injury
- Caloric requirement: Harris-Benedict x stress factor (1.5-2.0)
- Protein: 1.5-2 g/kg/day
- Vitamin C, zinc, selenium supplementation recommended
