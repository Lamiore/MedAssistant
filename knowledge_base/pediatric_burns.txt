SOURCE: Sheridan RL. Burns in children. Curr Probl Surg. 2004;41(9):694-762.
SOURCE: Palmieri TL. Pediatric Burn Resuscitation. Crit Care Clin. 2016;32(4):547-559.
TOPIC: Special Considerations for Pediatric Burn Management

WHY CHILDREN ARE DIFFERENT:
1. Body surface area to weight ratio is higher -> greater fluid and heat losses
2. Head is proportionally larger (up to 19% in newborns vs 9% in adults)
3. Legs are proportionally smaller at birth, grow with age
4. Thinner skin -> deeper burns from same heat exposure
5. Limited physiologic reserve
6. Risk of hypothermia is greater

LUND-BROWDER ADJUSTMENTS FOR CHILDREN:
The Lund-Browder modification for children uses the following rule:
- For each year of age ABOVE 1 year: subtract 1% from the head, add 0.5% to EACH leg
- Example: Age 5 -> Head = 19 - 5 = 14% total | Each leg = 13 + 2.5 = 15.5%

FLUID RESUSCITATION IN CHILDREN:
Modified Parkland (Galveston Formula):
  Resuscitation fluid: 3 mL x kg x TBSA%
  Maintenance fluid: 1500 mL/m2 BSA/day
  Use D5LR (5% dextrose in Lactated Ringer's) for maintenance
  Total = Resuscitation + Maintenance
  
ELECTROLYTE MONITORING:
- Children are at risk for hyponatremia (seizures)
- Check sodium every 4-6 hours in major burns
- Glucose monitoring every 2-4 hours (hypoglycemia risk)

BURN CENTER CRITERIA FOR CHILDREN:
Refer to pediatric burn center if:
- > 10% TBSA any depth
- > 2% TBSA full thickness
- Burns to face, hands, feet, genitalia
- Suspected child abuse
- Significant inhalation injury
- Electrical or chemical burns

PAIN MANAGEMENT NOTE:
Children require weight-based opioid dosing. Burns are extremely painful.
Do not withhold analgesia during assessment and resuscitation.
