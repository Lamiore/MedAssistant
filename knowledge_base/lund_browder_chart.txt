---
SOURCE: Lund CC, Browder NC. The estimation of areas of burns. Surg Gynecol Obstet. 1944;79:352-358.
TOPIC: Lund-Browder Chart - Gold Standard for Burn Surface Area Estimation

The Lund-Browder chart is the most accurate method for estimating total body surface area (TBSA) 
in burn patients because it accounts for the changing body proportions with age, 
particularly in pediatric patients.

AGE-DEPENDENT AREAS:
Area A (Half of head):
  Age 0:  9.5% | Age 1: 8.5% | Age 5: 6.5% | Age 10: 5.5% | Age 15: 4.5% | Adult: 4.5%

Area B (Half of one thigh):
  Age 0:  2.75% | Age 1: 3.25% | Age 5: 4.0% | Age 10: 4.25% | Age 15: 4.5% | Adult: 4.75%

Area C (Half of one lower leg):
  Age 0:  2.5% | Age 1: 2.5% | Age 5: 2.75% | Age 10: 3.0% | Age 15: 3.25% | Adult: 3.5%

FIXED AREAS (age-independent):
- Head (neck): 2%
- Anterior trunk: 13%
- Posterior trunk: 13%
- Right arm upper: 4%
- Right arm lower: 3%
- Right hand: 2.5%
- Left arm upper: 4%
- Left arm lower: 3%
- Left hand: 2.5%
- Genitalia: 1%
- Right foot: 3.5%
- Left foot: 3.5%

CLINICAL NOTE:
Only second-degree (partial thickness) and third-degree (full thickness) burns 
are included in TBSA calculation for fluid resuscitation purposes.
First-degree (superficial/epidermal) burns are EXCLUDED.

EXAMPLE:
Patient age 5, burns on anterior trunk + right arm upper + head:
- Anterior trunk: 13%
- Right arm upper: 4%
- Half head x 2: 6.5% x 2 = 13% -> but if only partially burned, estimate accordingly
Total TBSA = depends on extent of each burned area

WHY LUND-BROWDER IS SUPERIOR TO RULE OF NINES:
- Rule of Nines assigns 9% to the head for adults, but this is not appropriate for children
- A newborn's head accounts for 19% of TBSA (more than double the adult proportion)
- Legs grow proportionally larger from birth to adulthood
- Using Rule of Nines in children leads to significant underestimation of head burns
  and overestimation of leg burns, affecting fluid resuscitation accuracy

PRACTICAL APPLICATION:
The chart is typically used as a body diagram where clinicians shade in burned areas
and record the percentage for each region based on the patient's age.
The total TBSA is the sum of all second-degree and third-degree burned areas.
